Holy days of obligation

Holy days

    Every Sunday is a day of obligation. When January 1, August 15 or November 1 falls on a Saturday or a Monday, the obligation to attend Mass is lifted in the United States. In Hawaii, only Christmas and the Immaculate Conception are holy days of obligation.

    Sources and accuracy

    Worked out from the Church’s own rules, then checked against the USCCB.

    Every day is calculated in your browser from the General Roman Calendar, the Proper Calendar for the Dioceses of the United States of America, and the Universal Norms on the Liturgical Year and the Calendar: the date of Easter, the First Sunday of Advent, the Epiphany on the Sunday between January 2 and 8, the order of precedence between celebrations, and the rules for moving a solemnity when another day takes its place. Nothing is typed in by hand for one year, so the calendar works for any year.

    We compared it, day by day, with the USCCB’s published liturgical calendars for 2025, 2026 and 2027. For every day we could compare, the celebration, its rank, its liturgical color and the optional memorials agree.

    Your own calendar will differ in places. The patron saint and title of your parish, the anniversary of the dedication of your church and of your cathedral, and saints proper to your diocese or religious community are added locally and are not shown here. The Ascension follows the setting above. Saints added to the calendar in recent years appear from the year they were added; other past years are shown with today’s calendar.

    The readings are linked, not reproduced. They belong to the USCCB, and the link opens each day’s readings on its site.
