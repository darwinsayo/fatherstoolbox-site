## A letter from the parish office (Dry office humor)

### Run 1: A Few Gentle Reminders

Dear friends,

It has come to our attention that a number of you have questions. We have answers. Some of them are even correct.

Our office hours are 9 to 4, Monday through Friday, except at lunch. If the line is busy, it is because someone is asking where to park. Please be patient with us, and with the **popcorn machine**, which has opinions.

The lost and found now holds **14** umbrellas, a **sticky** **lawn mower**, and one glove that has been here since before the copier. If you are missing **folding chairs**, please come by. If you are not missing **folding chairs** but would like some, please also come by.

Bulletin deadlines are firm. When we say Wednesday at noon, we mean Wednesday at noon, not “Wednesday, give or take, depending on **bocce**.” Announcements that arrive late will run in the next bulletin, or in whichever one we can find them.

Finally, a reminder that the **supply closet** is not a storage unit, however much it has been acting like one. The **festive** **lawn mower** in the corner has been there since March. We have named it. We would like it to go home.

As we are told it is often said around here, **“We’ll talk about that at the parish council.”** We have it on a sticky note above the phone, next to the other **31** sticky notes.

*With great affection and a slight headache,  
The Parish Office (the ones with the keys)*

### Run 1: The Great Parish Showdown

One has not missed a potluck in **9** years. The other has never once brought the **pasta salad**. Neither will say why.

**Round one: Name that casserole.** Our contestants will taste **112** mystery dishes. First question: it’s **slightly damp**, it’s beige, and it has appeared at every potluck since the **pickleball** league began. Is it (A) **pasta salad**, (B) **deviled eggs** casserole, or (C) “I’d rather not say”? The answer is all three. Everybody gets a point.

**Round two: Speed stacking.** Each team has ninety seconds to stack as many **mismatched mittens** as they can. Points will be deducted for **yodeling**, for dropping a chair on a toe, and for “supervising.” Ooh, and Team Lot has one down. That is going to leave a mark.

**The lightning round.** Rapid fire! Who parks in the same spot every Sunday? Everyone! Who thinks the **microwave** works better if you hit it? Everyone! Who knows how the **squeak** started? No one! Who still owes the parish a casserole dish? Please see the freezer.

**The final question.** Here is our final question, worth **9** points. What is the most common thing heard in the **school gym**? Our survey says: **“We’ll talk about that at the parish council.”** Correct!

**The prizes.** Our winners take home a lifetime supply of **mismatched mittens**, a hand-lettered certificate, and the best parking spot, closest to the door, for one whole week. Runners-up receive a warm handshake and a coupon for **deviled eggs**.

*Thanks for playing, and drive safely!*

## The case of the missing thing (Noir detective)

### Run 1: The Case of the Missing Thermostat Remote

**The call.** It was a slow Tuesday at the parish office when the call came in. Somebody had lost the **thermostat remote**. Not just any **thermostat remote**: the **mysterious** one, with the good handle. I set down my **lemonade** and picked up my clipboard. This was going to be a long coffee hour.

**Clue one.** The first witness was the **possum** behind the **parish hall**. Cool customer. It wouldn’t say where it had been, but it did say it had seen **12** people near the supply closet, and that one of them was carrying **mismatched mittens**. In this parish, that tells you nothing. Everybody carries **mismatched mittens**.

**Clue two.** At the **parish hall**, I found a half-eaten **sheet cake**, a trail of **sensible** footprints, and a **buzz** I could not place. The sign-up sheet had been altered. Somebody had written “**humming**” next to their name, in handwriting I had seen before. The pen on the string was still attached. Whoever did this was careful, or lazy.

**Clue three.** My old partner used to say, **“Who parked in my spot?”** I always thought that was nonsense. Then I looked down. I looked at my left hand. I looked back at the clipboard in my right hand.

**The twist.** The **thermostat remote** was in my left hand the whole time. I had been carrying it since Sunday. I returned it to its rightful place, which was the **parish hall**, and nobody ever spoke of it again. The coffee was free and the donuts were gone.

*Case closed. Please return your folding chairs.*

## Countdown to the parish festival (Frazzled committee chair)

### Run 1: Ten Days to the Festival

**10 days to go.** The festival committee meets. It is agreed that this year will be simpler. The agenda has **3** items, and the first one is the **folding chair**.

**7 days to go.** The **donuts** order is placed. It is the wrong order. It is placed again. Somewhere, a volunteer begins **clipping coupons**, and no one is sure whose idea that was.

**3 days to go.** A **euchre** tournament is announced. Rules are posted. They are immediately argued about. The **overly cheerful** tent arrives. Nobody can find the instructions, and eleven people are sure they have put one up before.

**The day before.** Someone says, “It will all come together.” It will not come together until about 9:55 tomorrow morning. It never does until then. The forecast says rain. Everyone nods wisely and orders a second tent.

**Festival day.** Gates open. The first person in line asks when the **donuts** will be ready. The answer is ten minutes. It is gone five minutes after that. By noon there are **250** **lawn chairs** in the lost and found, and the **laminator** has made a **whistle** for the very first time.

**The day after.** The tent is down. The tables are folded. The leftover **donuts** goes home in bags. Father says, **“Let’s circle back to that.”** The committee schedules a meeting.

## House rules for the parish kitchen (Sign taped to the fridge)

### Run 1: House Rules for the Parish Kitchen

**Rule 1.** If you open it, close it. If you start it, finish it. If you break the **popcorn machine**, tell someone, preferably before the next potluck.

**Rule 2.** The **wobbly** **stapler** on the top shelf is not yours. It is not anyone’s. It has been there since before the renovation. Do not ask. Do not touch.

**Rule 3.** All **sheet cake** must be labeled with the cook’s name, the date, and an honest list of ingredients. We love you, but we have been surprised before.

**Rule 4.** Nobody may start **mopping** until the counters have been wiped down. This includes the person who brought the **goetta**. This includes you.

**Rule 5.** The coffee urn belongs to everyone, which means it belongs to no one, which means it is empty. If you take the last cup, make more. This is the oldest rule in the building.

**Rule 6.** Wash your own **mismatched mittens**. Dry your own **mismatched mittens**. Put your own **mismatched mittens** back where you found them. Where you found them is a mystery, so use your best judgment.

**Rule 7.** Any dispute about the thermostat will be settled by whoever is coldest, who will be ignored.

**Rule 8.** As our pastor likes to say, **“Let’s keep it moving.”** Please read it out loud, twice, before you leave.

*These rules were approved by the parish council by a vote of 50 to 250.*

## The one that wouldn’t quit (Storyteller first, point last)

### Run 1: The Generator That Wouldn’t Quit

Every parish has one: a **majestic** **generator** that arrived **7** years ago in the back of a **riding mower**, with a note that said “works great.” It did not work great. It worked the way a **very confident duck** works: eventually, and mostly on its own terms.

We tried everything. We tried new batteries, new oil, and **250** volunteers standing around giving advice. Somebody brought **pretzels**. Nothing helped. The **generator** made one long **clang** and went silent, and the whole **rectory kitchen** held its breath.

Then a very quiet volunteer, who had been **crossword puzzles** in the back all morning, set down their coffee, walked over, gave one small bolt a quarter turn, and said nothing. The **generator** started on the first pull. To this day, nobody knows how they knew.

**The point.** Here is what I take from it. Every parish is full of quiet people with the right small skill, who show up, turn one bolt, and go home. We should thank them. We should also stop giving advice. As I like to say, **“Let’s keep it moving.”**

*St. Joseph, patron of workers, pray for us.*
